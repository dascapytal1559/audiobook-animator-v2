# A66: The storyboard is one rough hand-drawn frame per declared shot on its own image track; the Storyboard section describes, drafts, and draws them through the ChatGPT subscription, with local Qwen as the fallback.

**Status.** Standing (2026-10-03; amended 2026-10-07 under A67 and A68, and 2026-10-09 under A69). Requested by the user: "describe shots at a chosen timestamp, and have AI generate a storyboard in hand-drawn style. Hand-drawn is deliberate because I'll choose a coherent style later but storyboard is our skeleton." Asked how it should behave, the user answered that the section shows the frame at the cursor, that the user writes the description with an optional AI draft to edit first, and that drawing goes "first through a ChatGPT model that can generate images as part of my subscription. Only if that fails do we go to local Qwen."

**Decision.** A *storyboard frame* is a declared shot (A65) on the `storyboard` image track (A60). Its anchor word is its identity; its description is the newest description take of that shot (A63), by any writer; its drawing is the image the `storyboard` track shows there. The frames are `storyboardFrames` in `packages/domain/src/storyboard.ts`: the track's shown shots that are anchored to a word, in timeline order, each holding until the track's next shot. A storyboard shot dragged off its word is a placement, not a frame, as A65 has it. The later, coherent style goes on another track over the same declared shots; the storyboard is drawn rough on purpose.

**The Storyboard section.** A fourth collapsible section of the one page (A63), between Video and Scenes, open at first. It follows the playhead. When a frame starts at or before the cursor (with the preview's seek tolerance), it shows that frame: the drawing on the left, and on the right its heading (frame number, opening words, start, each seeking), its description in an editable box, and Draft, Save, Draw or Redraw, and Revert. Otherwise it is empty, with a box to describe a new shot at the cursor's word: the word being spoken at the cursor, else the next word to start (clicking a sentence already puts the cursor at its first word). While a frame is shown and the cursor's word starts no frame, "New shot at …" switches the box to that word. Saving a new shot seeks to its first word, so a shot begun in a pause comes into view. Under both is a strip of every frame, each its drawing or "no drawing", the current one outlined, a running drawing marked ✎ and a failed one ⚠; a click seeks to the frame. What has been typed is kept per word while the playhead moves. *Amended by A68:* the frame is shown as a drafting space, Current (the narration it covers, its description, its drawing, read-only) beside Draft (a narration span, a request, a generated or edited description, and a draft drawing, each discardable), saved together as one snapshot; Draft, Save, Draw or Redraw, and Revert are gone. *Amended by A69:* the Draft column is replaced by the shot station, where the director describes the shot in their own words and works through kept versions until one is saved as the frame.

**Declaring a frame before its drawing.** Saving a new shot writes an image-less storyboard record (A31) placed at the word, then the description take. The image-less record is what declares the shot, so A65's consequence that "a description cannot come before any image of its shot" does not hold for the storyboard: the record is the shot, and the drawing comes later as a newer record in the same candidate group, which the newest-wins rule selects.

**A record may carry its own anchor.** `ShotRecord` gains two optional fields: `anchorWordId`, the word the writer placed the shot at, and `renderer`, what drew its image when it was generated. A record's own anchor places its shot at the word's effective start, like a decision anchor (A51), unless its decision anchors it to another word or places it by sample; dragging such a shot off its word writes a sample position, which detaches it, and dropping it back on its own word removes the override. A record anchored to a word the transcript lacks falls back to its `startSample` and is listed in `unresolvedAnchors` rather than failing the timeline, since an immutable record cannot be refused after it is written. `POST …/shots` takes `anchorWordId` (the shot then starts at the word's effective start unless a start is given) and `renderer`. The reason is who writes what: a drawing finishes in the background on the server while the browser owns `decisions.json` and replaces it wholesale on every autosave, so a decision the server wrote would be lost on the next save; a record the server publishes cannot be.

**Descriptions.** The section writes takes through the existing `POST …/scene-descriptions`. A take the person typed, or a draft they changed, names the writer `user` and, when it began as a draft, notes the model it was edited from; a draft saved word for word is the drafting model's take, with the prompt it answered. An edit appends a new take; nothing is overwritten. A Save that repeats the newest take is a no-op. The Scenes section still shows every take of a shot side by side and picks none; the Storyboard section reads the newest as the frame's working description, which is the one it draws from. This amends A63's "the editor generates nothing and picks nothing" for the storyboard only. *Amended by A69:* the editor no longer writes a take in the user's name; a frame's description comes from a shot station version and is the writer model's take.

**Draft.** *Removed by A69:* the route and its prompt are gone; the shot station's writer turns replace them. `POST …/storyboard/draft` with `{ anchorWordId }` runs one text turn of the Codex CLI under the ChatGPT login and answers `{ anchorWordId, text, model, prompt, seconds }`; nothing is recorded until the user saves. The prompt asks for 30 to 60 words of present-tense description (subject, action, setting, framing, light) for the frame beginning at the marked word, faithful to the passage, and quotes the story's title and the narration from 60 words before the word to 90 words from it, the word marked `[[like this]]`. The project's only other text-model route, OpenRouter for the A64 takes, is paid per call; the subscription route fits the user's answer for drawing, so drafts use it too.

**Drawing.** *Amended by A68:* `POST …/storyboard/draw` now takes `{ anchorWordId, startWordId, text }` and draws a draft drawing that waits for Save; the rest of this paragraph describes the route as first built, and still describes a first pass's drawings (A67). `POST …/storyboard/draw` with `{ anchorWordId }` needed a frame there with a description, refuses a second drawing of a frame already being drawn (409 `JobRunning`), and answers 202 with the job it started; `GET …/storyboard/jobs` lists the jobs. The drawing runs in a detached fiber in the server; the section polls the jobs every 1.5 s while one runs, shows which renderer is drawing and for how long, why one failed, and who drew it, and refetches the timeline when one finishes. Jobs live in the server's memory: a restart forgets them and loses a drawing in progress. The prompt asks for one storyboard frame drawn by hand, a rough pencil and ink sketch on off-white paper, greyscale only, loose gestural lines and light hatching, a wide 16:9 frame, simple unfinished figures, no colour or text; then the description, then the first 40 words of narration from the frame's word with the story's title, marked as context not to be written in the picture. *Amended by A67:* the prompt now asks for plain black line art on white, clean contour lines with no shading, hatching, texture, colour, or lettering; drawings wait for one of `storyboard.concurrentDraws` turns as `queued` jobs, and a job's `startedAt` is `requestedAt`.

- *Primary renderer:* ChatGPT's image tool through the Codex CLI under the user's ChatGPT login, so each drawing is subscription usage rather than a per-image charge. The exact invocation, the prompt on stdin:

  ```sh
  codex exec --ignore-user-config --ephemeral --skip-git-repo-check --sandbox read-only --json \
    --cd <fresh empty temp dir> --model gpt-6-astra -c 'model_reasoning_effort="low"' -c 'web_search="disabled"' \
    --disable shell_tool --disable apps --disable plugins --disable browser_use --disable browser_use_external \
    --disable computer_use --disable in_app_browser --disable hooks --enable image_generation -
  ```

  A draft is the same with `--disable image_generation` in place of `--enable`. The `--json` events name the thread (`thread.started`) but not the image; Codex saves it as `<CODEX_HOME>/generated_images/<thread id>/exec-<call id>.png`, `CODEX_HOME` defaulting to `~/.codex`, and the newest PNG there is copied into the new record. Codex's own copy is left where Codex keeps it.
- *Fallback:* local Qwen Image 2.1 through MFLUX's `mflux-generate-qwen-2.1`, with A64's recipe (8-bit weights, 1024x576, 40 steps, guidance 1.0, seed 42), the prompt in a file; tried only when the primary fails for any reason (not installed, not logged in, refused, no image, timed out after 300 s, out of quota), one drawing at a time, with a 900 s limit.
- *Record:* each drawing is a new record on the `storyboard` track, anchored to the frame's word at its effective start when the drawing finishes, with `renderer` `codex-chatgpt` or `qwen-image-2.1`, the label "Storyboard frame", the exact prompt that renderer was given, and notes naming the model and thread or command, the seconds it took, the image's size, and, after a fallback, why the primary failed.

**What a prompt may carry.** Only the description, the story's title, and the narration excerpt. Codex runs in a fresh empty directory with a read-only sandbox, without the user's Codex configuration (so no MCP servers, notifications, or the full-access sandbox that configuration sets), with its shell, web search, browser, computer use, apps, plugins, and hooks switched off, persists no session, and reads the prompt from stdin rather than its command line. Story files, paths under `data/`, and keys are never sent. A read-only sandbox still lets a turn read files, and the Codex build keeps a JavaScript orchestration tool and sub-agents that these flags do not remove; the turn is told not to read files or run commands, and the empty working directory gives it nothing nearby. With the shell off, a probe asked to run `ls /` reported it could not and still drew its image (49 s).

**Settings.** *Amended by A69:* `codex.model` is now `codex.writerModel`, for every text turn, and `codex.drawModel`, for drawing; `draftTimeoutMs` is `writeTimeoutMs`. The `storyboard` section of a run file, defaults in `storyboardDefaults` (`src/modules/storyboard/contracts.ts`): the Codex executable, model, reasoning effort, optional home, and draw and draft time limits; the fallback's command line with `{promptFile}` and `{output}`, and its time limit; and the excerpt lengths. The editor server prints them with its other settings.

**Evidence.** Measured on 2026-10-03 against a scratch copy of Understand behind a separate editor server, with the browser driving the section; the user's data and running editor were not touched.

The default command line follows MFLUX's published interface and has not been run here.

**Consequences.** A frame's identity is its word, so its drawings and descriptions follow timing edits and stay with the word if the frame moves (A65). An explicit selection of an older drawing in a frame's group still wins over a newer drawing (A25), so a redraw then does not show until that selection is cleared. Drawing is subscription usage and is capped by the subscription's limits; a refusal or quota error falls back to Qwen.

**Amends.** A51 (a record may carry its own anchor), A63 (a fourth section; the editor drafts, draws, and writes description takes for the storyboard), and A65 (an image-less storyboard record declares a shot before any drawing).
