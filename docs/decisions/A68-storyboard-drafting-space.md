# A68: The Storyboard section is a drafting space: the frame as saved beside a draft of it, saved as one snapshot of narration, description, and drawing; nothing is overwritten.

**Status.** Standing (2026-10-07). Requested by the user: "we want current description and we want current transcription as a blob of text … And then we also want a new box for new shots as well, just so we can have the old and the new kind of side by side … somewhere we can tell the transcript of what our request is, as well as a generator shot description and then a generator shot. And we can choose to either discard like a generator shot or discard a generator description or discard a transcription. Or we can also choose to save it, which takes a snapshot of like the transcription, takes a snapshot of the shot description and takes a snapshot of the shots as well. So a kind of … drafting space." Asked whether "transcription" meant the narration the shot covers or a log of requests, the user answered: "Transcription means the narration text that the shot covers."

**Decision.** For the storyboard frame at the cursor (A66), the Storyboard section shows two columns, stacked when the window is narrow. **Current**, on the left, is the frame as saved and is read-only: the narration it covers as a block of text, its description (the newest take, naming its writer), and its drawing. **Draft**, on the right, is what the frame would become: the narration it would cover, a request telling the AI what to change, a description, and a drawing. Each part of the draft starts as the current one, can be changed or generated on its own, and can be discarded on its own, which returns that part to the current one. **Save** commits the whole draft as one snapshot, which becomes the frame. "New shot at …" opens the same draft for a shot that does not exist yet, at the cursor's word; its Current column says no shot starts there yet. The strip of frames and the first pass (A67) stay below.

**The narration a shot covers.** A shot covers the words from its anchor word to the last word that starts before the storyboard track's next shot, counted over the effective words in order of start (`spanBounds` in `packages/domain/src/storyboard.ts`). In the draft, that span is a highlighted run inside a passage of the narration around it; clicking a word sets the span's start, then its end, as the "click a word to set its start / end" toggle says. The span may give words to its neighbours or take words from them, but it may not start at or before the storyboard shot before it, nor reach the shot after it, since either would swallow that shot (`judgeSpan`); the passage offers only words inside those bounds, and the server refuses a span outside them. To make a shot run longer into the next one, move the next shot's start instead.

- *A moved start.* Since a frame's identity is its word (A65, A66), a shot whose start moves is saved as a new storyboard record at the new start word, carrying the drawing (the draft drawing, or a copy of the current one) and the description (the draft's, or the current take carried over unchanged, naming its original writer and noting where it came from). The shots of the frame at the old word are then hidden by the editor, through its own decisions: the server names them in the snapshot's answer and writes no decisions, because the editor owns `decisions.json` and replaces it wholesale on every autosave (A66). The old records and takes stay where they are, hidden, as history. Words between the old and the new start go to the shot before, or to the opening before any frame; the draft says so before saving. A frame whose drawing a first pass has queued or is running cannot be moved until that drawing ends, since it would land on the old word and declare it again.
- *A drawn-in end.* When the drafted end stops before where the shot would hold, the snapshot also declares a new, empty frame (an image-less storyboard record) at the word after the end, so those words keep a shot of their own rather than staying with this one or going to a neighbour unannounced. When that word is the frame's own old start, as when a shot is moved one span earlier and ends just before where it began, the old frame is kept as that shot instead of being hidden and declared again.

**Request and generated description.** `POST …/storyboard/draft` (A66) now takes `{ anchorWordId, endWordId?, current?, request? }`: the excerpt marks the whole drafted span `[[like this]]` and runs on at least past it, the prompt quotes the description in the draft (or the current one) as the shot's description now, and the request as what the director asks for, to follow while keeping what it does not change. **Generate description** sends the drafted span, the draft's description, and the request; the answer lands in the draft's description box, editable. The writer rules of A66 hold: a model's draft saved word for word is that model's take with the prompt it answered, and anything edited is the user's, noting the model it began from.

**Generated drawing as a draft drawing.** **Generate drawing** draws the description in the draft, not the saved one, with A66's and A67's renderers, prompt, and turns, and the result becomes the shot's *draft drawing*: never shown as the frame's drawing, in the preview, or on the timeline until it is saved. `POST …/storyboard/draw` now takes `{ anchorWordId, startWordId, text }` (the shot the draft belongs to, the word the narration excerpt starts at, and the description to draw) and queues a job of kind `draft`; it no longer publishes a record, and the section's old Draw and Redraw buttons are gone, replaced rather than kept beside it. A first pass's drawings are still published straight onto the storyboard as jobs of kind `frame` (A67). A job names the record or the draft it wrote; a frame and its draft can be drawn at the same time, but not two draft drawings of one shot.

- *Where a draft drawing lives.* `<story>/storyboard-drafts/<ulid>/draft.json` with its image beside it, published by one rename like a shot record (`src/modules/storyboard/drafts.ts`), pinned to the clip, and naming the shot it belongs to, the description it was drawn from, and its renderer, prompt, and notes. A shot has at most one: a newer draft drawing removes the one before. Saving copies its image into the new record and removes it; discarding removes it. A draft drawing is a working file, not evidence, so these removals are the only deletions this decision makes; anything saved is kept. `GET …/storyboard/drafts` lists them, `GET …/storyboard/drafts/<id>/image` serves one, and `POST …/storyboard/drafts/discard` with `{ anchorWordId }` removes a shot's.
- *What a reload keeps.* A finished draft drawing is on disk, so a reload or a server restart keeps it; a drawing still running is lost on a server restart, as A66 has it for every job. The request, the drafted description with the draft it began as, and the drafted span are kept by the browser, per story and per shot, so a reload in the same browser keeps them; another browser or a cleared site storage does not see them.

**Save.** `POST …/storyboard/snapshot` with `{ frameWordId, startWordId, endWordId, description, draftId }` (`frameWordId` null for a new shot, `description` null to keep the current one, `draftId` null to keep the current drawing) judges the span against the storyboard as it stands and writes, under the story's take lock:

1. a new storyboard record at the start word, labelled "Storyboard frame", when the drawing changed or the shot is new or moved, with the draft drawing's image and renderer and prompt, or a copy of the current drawing's, or no image;
2. a description take at the start word, when the description changed or the shot moved; a repeat of a take the word already has is no take, as A66 has it;
3. the empty frame after a drawn-in end.

It answers 201 with what it wrote and the shot ids to hide, and refuses a save that changes nothing. A save that changes only the description writes only the take, so an unchanged drawing is not copied into a new record each time; a snapshot is the set of what one save wrote, and the shot's history is its records and takes, each with its time. Nothing is overwritten. Save is enabled when any part differs, and always for a new shot, since saving declares it.

**Consequences.** Every change to a frame now passes through the draft and one Save, so a drawing no longer lands on the storyboard the moment it finishes; it is one more step than A66's Draw, in exchange for seeing old and new side by side before choosing. Moving a shot leaves hidden records and takes on its old word, as A65 already does for any shot moved off its word, and shows them again if a shot is declared there. The hide that completes a move is the editor's autosave after the server's write; if that save fails, both frames show until it succeeds, and the header reports the failure. A take the user restores word for word to an older take of the same writer is refused as a repeat and the newer take stays the description, as under A66.

**Amends.** A66: the Storyboard section's layout and actions (the drafting space replaces the single editable box with Draft, Save, Draw or Redraw, and Revert), the draft route's request, and the draw route, which now draws a draft drawing. A67: drawing jobs carry a kind.
