# A67: A first pass proposes a beat's shots, declares them, describes them, and draws them as plain line drawings; nothing that exists is overwritten.

**Status.** Standing (2026-10-07; amended the same day under A68: drawing jobs carry a kind, `frame` for a first pass's drawings and `draft` for the drafting space's). Requested by the user: "What we need next is a first pass. Both in prompt, like a picture description, as well as in image form. And we want that at every sentence or whatever the AI deems deserves a shot … Let's try to do that for Understand, scene one, beat one. The first pass is going to give us a jumpstart." On style, of the A64 shot-one images by GPT and Qwen: "They're both really good in terms of the shots. But I kind of just want less colors and style attached to it, just so I can then kind of apply my own styles later."

**Decision.** A *first pass* plans one beat or scene of the story map (A62): an AI pass reads the section's narration and proposes its shots, then, once the user agrees, each proposed shot becomes a storyboard frame (A66) with a description and a drawing. The map still declares no shot on its own (A65); the pass is asked for, shown, and confirmed by the user, and what it declares is ordinary word anchors.

**Planning.** `POST …/storyboard/first-pass/plan` with `{ sectionId }` takes a section of kind `beat` or `scene`; an act or chapter is refused as too large to plan in one turn. One text turn of the Codex CLI under the ChatGPT login, with A66's flags and the configured model, is given the story's title, the section's title and summary, about 60 words of narration before it as context, and the section's narration one sentence per numbered line (sentences as A61 derives them). It is asked to decide where each shot begins: a sentence gets at most one shot of its own, consecutive sentences that stay on one picture are merged, and a long sentence is split only where the picture clearly changes; the first shot begins at the section's first word. Shots already declared in the section are quoted to it to keep. For each shot it answers `opens`, the shot's first four to eight words copied from the narration, and `description`, 30 to 60 words of present-tense description of subject, action, setting, framing, and light, in A66's draft style; the reply is one JSON object. Each quote is placed on the word it begins at, searched as whole words after the previous shot's word so shots stay in order and a repeated phrase lands on its next use; when the whole quote is not found, its first three words are tried. A quote that still cannot be placed is reported as unmatched and left out. Shots already declared in the section that the model left out stay in the plan without new text. The answer is the plan: each shot's word, start, and proposed text, judged against the storyboard as it stands, the unmatched quotes, and the model and exact prompt. Nothing is written.

**Judging a shot; nothing is overwritten.** At each shot's word: `frame` is `new` when no storyboard frame starts there, else `kept`; `description` is `kept` when the shot has any description take, `new` when it has none and the model proposed one, `none` otherwise; `drawing` is `kept` when the frame shows a drawing, `busy` when one is queued or running, `new` when there is a description to draw from, `none` otherwise. A shot declared on another track (A65) has no storyboard frame yet, so it gets one, keeps its descriptions, and is drawn from the newest of them.

**Applying.** `POST …/storyboard/first-pass` takes `{ sectionId, model, prompt, shots: [{ anchorWordId, text }] }`, the plan as proposed or trimmed, and judges every shot again against the storyboard as it stands then; a word outside the section or named twice is refused. It declares each new frame with an image-less storyboard record at the word (A66), labelled "Storyboard frame", noting the first pass; records each new description as the planning model's take, with the plan's prompt and a note naming the pass and the shot's place in it; then queues a drawing of every frame marked `new`. It holds the story's take lock while it writes, and answers 202 with a fresh `firstPassId`, the shots as judged, and the queued jobs, each naming the pass. Run twice over the same beat, the second pass keeps everything and draws nothing.

**Drawings in turn.** Every storyboard drawing, from the Draw button or a first pass, now waits for one of `storyboard.concurrentDraws` turns (default 2) in the server, so a pass of twenty-five shots runs two at a time rather than all at once against the subscription. A job is `queued` from `requestedAt` until its turn, then `running`, then `done` or `failed`; `startedAt` on the job is renamed `requestedAt`, with no alias, since each attempt keeps its own start. A drawing reads its frame's newest description when its turn comes, so a description edited while it waits is the one drawn. The jobs live in the server's memory, as A66 has it.

**Plain line drawings.** The one drawing prompt, `sketchPrompt` in `src/modules/storyboard/index.ts`, used for every storyboard drawing, no longer asks for a rough pencil and ink sketch on off-white paper in greyscale with loose gestural lines and light hatching. It asks for plain black line art on a solid, opaque white background drawn by hand, simple clean contour lines of even weight; no colour, no grey tones, no shading or hatching, no texture, no lettering; simple figures and props as plain outlines, setting staging, framing, and composition, not style; a wide 16:9 frame without text, captions, speech bubbles, panel numbers, or borders; and, where the description names colours, materials, or lighting, only the shapes and where they are. The description and the narration excerpt follow as before. The prompt stays one constant so the storyboard reads as one skeleton, and the user's own style can go on another track later.

**In the editor.** The Storyboard section gains a bar above its strip: **First pass** of the beat or scene holding the playhead (the innermost one in the map's current chain), disabled outside any or without a map. It asks for the plan, then shows it in place: one line of counts (shots, new frames, new descriptions, drawings to make, and what is kept), every shot with its opening words and start (each seeking), what happens to its frame, description, and drawing, each new description in full, and any quote left out; **Apply** carries it out and **Discard** drops it. A line beside the button counts the newest pass's drawings as drawn, failed, drawing, and waiting; the strip marks a waiting frame with "…". The browser's own confirm dialog is not used.

**From the command line.** `animator storyboard first-pass --story <id> --section <id> [--run <file>] [--dry-run]` plans in the same process, prints the plan with `--dry-run`, and otherwise applies it and waits while the drawings run `concurrentDraws` at a time, with progress on stderr and the plan and what was done on stdout. It needs no editor server, writes the same records and takes, and its drawings are not in a running server's jobs list; the editor sees the records through its change notice. A take written by the CLI while the editor server writes one to the same story could lose one of them, since the take lock is per process, so the two are not run on the same story at once.

**Settings.** `storyboard.codex.planTimeoutMs` (300 s), `storyboard.concurrentDraws` (2), and `storyboard.excerpt.planWordsBefore` (60) join A66's settings.

**Evidence.** Measured on 2026-10-07 against a scratch copy of Understand behind a separate editor server on port 63641, the browser driving the section; the user's data and running editor were not touched. On this machine a shell whose PATH puts `~/.local/bin` first resolves `codex` to `0.147.0`, which the service now refuses for `gpt-6-astra` ("requires a newer version of Codex"); `~/.vite-plus/bin/codex` is the `0.157.1` README names and answers `gpt-6-astra`. The browser pass therefore ran with a scratch run file setting `storyboard.codex.model` to `gpt-5.6-sol`, which `0.147.0` accepts, and the command-line pass with `storyboard.codex.executable` set to `0.157.1` and the default model.

The drawings are clean black contours with no colour, grey, or shading. Seven of the browser pass's 25 PNGs came back with a transparent background rather than white, so the prompt now asks for a solid, opaque white background (the three later drawings were opaque), and the editor shows storyboard drawings on white wherever it shows them (the section, the preview, and the Scenes takes); the records keep what the renderer made.

**Consequences.** A first pass is subscription usage on the scale of one text turn plus one drawing per shot; at two at a time, a 25-shot beat took eight minutes. The plan is only as good as one turn's reading, so the user confirms it in full before anything is written, and every take it writes names the model and the prompt. A frame's identity stays its word (A65, A66), so takes and drawings follow timing edits.

**Amends.** A66: the drawing prompt (plain line drawings instead of rough greyscale sketches), the drawing jobs (queued in turns, `requestedAt`), and the settings.
