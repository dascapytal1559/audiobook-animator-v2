# A69: The Storyboard section's working column is a shot station: the director describes a shot in their own words, gets several directions each drawn, and picks, mixes, edits, or sets motion, each step a kept version; one version is saved as the frame.

**Status.** Standing (2026-10-09). Requested by the user on 2026-10-09 as a voice-led loop for making a shot: listen to the narration, say what you see, get a few directions back as pictures, choose or combine them, and change one by saying what to change, without anything earlier being lost. The request and its planning are private and are described here in the project's words. The user rarely used the drafting space (A68) and left its replacement open, so the station replaces A68's Draft column and its draft drawings rather than sitting beside them.

**Decision.** The Storyboard section (A66) keeps **Current**, the frame at the cursor as saved, and its first pass (A67) and strip of frames. Its working column is the *shot station*, which works on one shot: a storyboard frame's word, or the word a new shot is begun at ("New shot at …", as in A68). In it, in order:

1. **Narration.** The span the shot covers, picked word by word within its neighbours exactly as A68 has it (`spanBounds`, `judgeSpan`), and **Play**, which plays that span and puts the playhead back where it was. The section holds its shot while the narration plays, and follows the playhead again when it stops, so playing a span never swaps the station under the director.
2. **Your description.** A plain text box for the director's own words: typed, or pasted by a dictation app, which is how the user dictates; no microphone is built in. **Get N directions** (four unless another number from one to six is picked, remembered per browser) asks the writer model for that many directions.
3. **Versions.** Every version of the shot, numbered in order, each with a thumbnail and what made it ("4 directions", "picked 2 of v1", "edited v3: …", "mixed 1 + 3 of v1", "motion of v4: …", "2 directions, revising v1"). One is open: the newest, unless another is chosen, and the choice is remembered per browser. Opening an earlier version shows it as it was, its images included.
4. **The open version.** Its directions as cards, each its picture, a few words naming the idea, a short description of what the camera sees, its motion, and its image prompt folded away. A version of several directions offers **Pick** on each card and **Select**: select one and say what to change, or select several and say what to take from each (or nothing) to **Mix** them. A version of one direction offers the same change box, **Motion** (the model's suggested movements, **still**, or the director's own words), and **Save vN as the frame**.

**A direction** is `{ title, description, prompt, motion, motionOptions }`: a few words, one or two sentences of what the camera sees, a self-contained prompt of 40 to 90 words for an image model (subject and action, setting, framing and camera angle, light, palette, mood; people described by how they look, not by name; no lettering), the movement the director asked for or null for a still frame, and three movements that would suit it.

**A version** is what one step made, kept at `<story>/shot-station/versions/<ulid>/version.json`, pinned to the clip, published by one rename like a shot record (A30), and never changed: the shot it was made for (`shotWordId`), the narration span it was made for, its parent version, its action and inputs, the writer turn (model, exact prompt, seconds) or null, and its directions. The actions:

- `describe`: the director's words as given, and how many directions were asked for. Its parent is the version whose description the director revised (**edit this description** loads it into the box), else none.
- `pick`: one direction of the parent, carried unchanged with its image; no model is asked.
- `mix`: two or more directions of the parent combined as the instruction says; the writer answers one direction.
- `edit`: one direction of the parent changed as the instruction says ("make the sky red"); the writer is told to change only that and to keep the prompt's wording elsewhere, so the director never describes the shot from the start again.
- `motion`: the parent's one direction carried with another motion, or none; no model is asked.

A carried direction names the version and direction it came from (`carried`), followed back to the one that was drawn, so its image is that one's and the model credited for its text is the one that wrote it. Which versions a shot shows are those made for it or begun at its word, with every version they descend from (`versionsForShot`), so a shot saved at a moved start keeps its history.

**Writing.** Every station text turn is one Codex turn under the ChatGPT login with A66's flags, by the *writer model*: `storyboard.codex.writerModel`, the single setting for which model writes, which also writes a first pass's plan (A67). Drawing has its own setting, `storyboard.codex.drawModel`; both default to `gpt-6-astra`, and the old `storyboard.codex.model` is gone, replaced rather than aliased. The prompts quote the story's title and the narration with the shot's span marked `[[like this]]`, the director's words in triple quotes as given (dictation may ramble), and, for a mix or an edit, the description the version descends from and the directions concerned; the reply is a JSON object, read between its first `{` and last `}`. A turn that fails, or answers without the JSON, is a 502 `WriteFailed` naming what it said, and writes no version. `storyboard.codex.writeTimeoutMs` (180 s) replaces A68's draft limit, and `storyboard.excerpt.spanWordsBefore` and `spanWordsAfter` (60 and 90) replace its draft excerpt lengths.

**Drawing.** Each direction a describe, mix, or edit writes anew is drawn at once in the background, with A66's renderers: Codex's image tool under the ChatGPT login, then local Qwen Image 2.1 only when that fails. The picture is asked for from the image prompt as written, as one frame of a wide 16:9 film with no text (`framePrompt`). Unlike a first pass's frames, it is not a plain line drawing (A67): the director shapes colour, light, and style here, so the prompt's are kept. Every try is a record of its own at `<story>/shot-station/images/<ulid>/image.json`, with the image beside it, or, when both renderers fail, every renderer's reason instead, so a failed picture stays visibly failed after a reload or a restart. A card is drawn (its image), drawing or waiting (the job's line), failed (why, with **Draw again**), or, when no try is recorded and none runs, as after a restart lost one, not drawn (with **Draw again**). A direction that has an image keeps it: drawing again is refused, and a different picture is an edit. `storyboard.concurrentDraws` is now 4, so a description's four directions draw together; a first pass's frames draw four at a time too.

**Saving.** **Save vN as the frame** is A68's snapshot with the version in place of a draft: `POST …/storyboard/snapshot` takes `{ frameWordId, startWordId, endWordId, versionId }`, `versionId` null to change only the span. The version must have one direction, drawn. The new storyboard record at the start word carries the image, its renderer, its exact prompt, and `stationVersionId`, a new optional field of every shot record naming the version a frame was saved from, with notes naming the version and its motion; the description take there is the direction's description as the writer model's take, with the turn's prompt and notes giving the version, the title, the image prompt, and the motion. A version that is already the frame's (the frame's record names it) saves nothing more; a moved start, a drawn-in end, and the retired old frame are exactly A68's. Nothing is overwritten: the records and takes already there stay, and every version and image try is kept. A save with no version and an unchanged span is refused as before.

**What goes.** A68's Draft column (its Request box, **Generate description**, **Generate drawing**, a typed description, and per-part discards), its draft drawings under `storyboard-drafts/`, and the routes `POST …/storyboard/draft`, `POST …/storyboard/draw`, `GET …/storyboard/drafts`, `GET …/storyboard/drafts/:id/image`, and `POST …/storyboard/drafts/discard` are removed, with job kind `draft`; the editor no longer writes takes in the user's name, since the station's words are inputs and its descriptions are the writer model's. A story's leftover `storyboard-drafts/` directory is no longer read; it was a working file, not evidence, and is left where it is.

**Routes.** `GET …/storyboard/station` answers every version and image try of the story, oldest first, each image with its URL. `POST …/storyboard/station/versions` takes `{ shotWordId, startWordId, endWordId, parentId, action }` and answers 201 with the version and the drawings it started; a pick or motion choice answers at once, a describe, mix, or edit after the writer (400 for a word not in the transcript, an end before the start, an action the parent cannot take, or a pick, mix, edit, or motion without a parent). `POST …/storyboard/station/draw` takes `{ versionId, direction }` and draws again a direction with no image (202; 400 when it has one; 409 while it is being drawn). `GET …/storyboard/station/images/:id/image` serves an image. Jobs are `GET …/storyboard/jobs`, now of kind `frame` or `station`, a station job naming its version, direction, and image.

**In the browser.** What the director is writing, the version it revises, the span picked, and the version open are kept per story and per shot by the browser, so a reload keeps a dictation in progress; another browser does not see them, but every version and image is on disk. Cmd/Ctrl+Enter sends the description or the change.

**Consequences.** A shot is made in one place, with every step kept, and its frame names the version it came from; the scratch history behind a frame is reopened by version, not by browsing records. A round of four directions costs one text turn and four drawings of subscription usage, about a minute and a half in all; a pick or a motion choice costs nothing. Frames saved from the station are in colour on the storyboard track beside a first pass's line drawings; the storyboard skeleton (A66) is no longer all one style, and a coherent style can still go on another track later. Motion is recorded with each version and in a saved record's notes; no animation is generated. Side-by-side comparison of versions, other image models, and animation are later work.
