# A62: A story map names a story's subjects and structure as word ranges; the editor explores it read-only.

**Status.** Standing (2026-09-18). Requested by the user as a story explorer showing characters, objects, and locations, and the story broken into chapters or acts. This revisits the world model withdrawn on 2026-09-09, scoped to what the explorer shows.

**Decision.** A story may carry `story-map.json` at the top of its directory, beside `decisions.json`. It is pinned to the clip identity and holds two lists. *Subjects* are the characters, locations, objects, and motifs that recur, each with a stable id, a name, an optional description, the *mentions* where the narration names or clearly refers to it, and optional reference images kept inside the story directory. *Sections* are acts, chapters, scenes, and beats. Every mention and section is an inclusive range of transcript word ids, never samples: the map stays valid under timing edits and is resolved onto effective word timing on read, as shot anchors are (A51). Sections nest by containment; any two must be disjoint or one must contain the other, and none may cover exactly the same words. Coverage may be partial while a map is in progress.

Agents and scripts write the file; the editor and the `story map` verb read it. The editor does not edit it. The server refuses a map that is unpinned, off the transcript, or names a missing or foreign image, naming the file and the rule; an absent map is a normal state the explorer shows as "no map yet". Its byte ceiling is `editor.limits.maxStoryMapBytes`.

**Explorer.** A second view of the editor, chosen in the header and carried in the URL as `view=explorer`. It takes the whole area under the header; the preview, transport, timeline, and shot panel belong to the timeline view. The audio element and playhead are shared, so a seek in the explorer is where the timeline resumes. The structure column lists sections in narration order, indented by depth, with the current chain highlighted the way shots hold: at each depth the last section that has started. Sections with children start collapsed so every act reads at a glance; the chain under the playhead opens itself. A section without children can unfold its transcript passage, built from the transcript elements so punctuation is as written, with each word seeking the playhead and the current word marked. The cast column groups subjects by kind with their first image and owns the column until a subject is selected; then the list keeps the top and the detail takes the bottom, each scrolling on its own, until the detail is closed or the card clicked again. The divider between the columns and the one between list and detail drag; both splits are browser view preferences, never part of the story. The detail shows the description, images, the sections the subject appears in, and every mention with its words, each seeking the playhead. The explorer resolves ranges with the words the timeline shows, so pending manual timing edits apply.

**Why.** The pilot's analysis, continuity index, and shot plan already reason in these terms (recurring identities per shot, evidenced beats) but as prose and run-local JSON keyed to a retired transcript. One schema in the domain package gives them a durable home the browser, the server, and future planning scripts share (A58), without inventing a fictional world beyond what the shots exercise. Word ids are the only story axis that survives alignment and manual edits.

**Scope.** No editing of the map in the browser, no automated extraction, and no timeline lane for sections yet. Shot records do not reference subjects; that link is a later decision.
